# RESEARCH

Last updated: 2026-10-25 (venue verification)

This file holds the search protocol and the literature matrix. Every entry was opened from the primary source (the paper's own PDF or publisher page) and the matrix was filled from that text, paraphrased. Status vocabulary: `READ` means the whole paper was read; `PARTIAL` means only some sections were read, and the sections are listed in the status cell; `TO READ` means located but not read; `NR` means not retrieved or could not be opened. A `PARTIAL` paper may be cited only for statements that rest on the sections listed. In the Session 1 review of the Session 0 papers none stayed `READ`: they were read selectively (abstract, method, protocol, result tables, limitations), so R-001 to R-018 are all `PARTIAL` with their sections stated. They become `READ` only after a full read. R-019 to R-067 carry the status assigned by the Session 1 stream that read them (C1, C2 or C3; the mapping is in docs/_agents/MERGE_MAP.md). After the merge the matrix holds 19 `READ` and 48 `PARTIAL` entries; `NR` items are in their own table below.

Rule for numbers: each headline result names the protocol it was measured under. In-dataset numbers (train and test drawn from the same dataset) and cross-dataset numbers (train on one dataset, test on another) are never mixed in one cell. A number from a paper is not our result and is never reported as ours.

## Search protocol

- Date of this search: 2026-10-24 (Session 0).
- Sources used to find papers: a general web search engine (queries below); arXiv for downloading PDFs by identifier; the CVF open-access site; the Frontiers and EURASIP publisher sites.
- How papers were read: the PDF was downloaded to a scratch folder outside the repository, converted to text with pdftotext, and read section by section (abstract, dataset or method, experimental protocol, results tables, limitations). The Frontiers article was read from its publisher HTML page. PDFs are not stored in the repository (size and copyright).
- Identifier check: every arXiv identifier was checked against the title page of the downloaded file. One identifier written from memory (2009.14232) turned out to be an astrophysics paper and was discarded; the intended paper was found by search and checked (see STATUS.md, Mistakes and corrections).
- Query terms used (some in several variants): deepfake benchmark dataset; FaceForensics++; Celeb-DF; DFDC dataset; Face X-ray; FakeCatcher biological signals; rPPG heart rate deepfake; deepfake preserve pulse signal modern generators; remote photoplethysmography deepfake detection 2023 2024; audio-visual deepfake detection FakeAVCeleb; audio-visual dissonance deepfake; joint audio-visual deepfake detection; AASIST audio anti-spoofing; ASVspoof 2019 database; universal fake image detector CLIP; DeepfakeBench; calibration of modern neural networks; transfer learning ImageNet fine-tuning; deepfake survey.
- Inclusion: peer-reviewed or arXiv papers on deepfake or forgery detection, deepfake datasets and benchmarks, audio anti-spoofing, audio-visual detection, physiological-signal detection, generalisation and evaluation methodology, calibration and uncertainty, transfer learning, and surveys, that I could open from a primary source.
- Exclusion: news articles, blogs, press releases and vendor pages as evidence (they are used only to find primary papers); papers I could not open (marked `NR`); papers whose content I have not read (marked `TO READ`).
- Session 1 (2026-10-24): three literature streams ran in parallel, one per theme. C1: audio-visual, lip-sync and audio anti-spoofing. C2: generalising visual detectors, fine-tuning of pretrained backbones, generated-image detection, evaluation methodology, calibration and uncertainty. C3: rPPG on current generators, extraction methods, compression and skin-tone effects, surveys. Each stream located papers by web search, downloaded PDFs with curl, converted them with pdftotext, and checked every arXiv identifier against the downloaded title page. Their raw reports are kept in docs/_agents/ (C1.md, C2.md, C3.md).
- Verification by the integrator before merging: the title page of every downloaded PDF listed by C1, C2 and C3 was re-opened and matched the cited paper; a sample of headline numbers was re-checked against the paper text (C1: wav2vec 2.0, LAV-DF, Zhou and Lim, ASVspoof 2021, In-the-Wild, AV-Deepfake1M; C2: the Effort fine-tuning numbers, the file-format bias number, the oracle-threshold table; C3: McDuff compression, Yang et al. heart-rate and detection numbers, DeepRhythm, Harraq and Aldwairi (second paper), the POS window length). All sampled numbers were found. Numbers not sampled have not been re-checked by the integrator.
- Coverage after Session 0: 18 papers opened (all PARTIAL, see above; R-001 to R-018). Coverage after Session 1: 67 papers (R-001 to R-067). Areas covered: face-forgery datasets (R-001 to R-003), visual detectors (R-004, R-017, R-011), physiological-signal detectors and their test on modern generators (R-005 to R-010), audio anti-spoofing (R-012, R-013), audio-visual (R-014, R-015), calibration (R-016), transfer learning (R-018). Not yet covered: surveys (two located, not read), audio-visual joint training, uncertainty beyond calibration, fine-tuning methods such as adapters, and the newest (2025 and 2026) image detectors.

## Notes that bear on decisions

1. Reported numbers in this literature rarely share a protocol. R-001, R-005, R-007 and R-015 report in-dataset results; R-002, R-004, R-011 and R-017 report cross-dataset or cross-generator results; and these are far lower. Example: the same family of detectors scores above 94 mean AUC within FF++ and below 80 across datasets in R-017.
2. Plain pretrained backbones are competitive when the recipe is matched (R-017), and a frozen pretrained feature space with a linear head generalised well across generators for still images (R-011). Both are relevant to the MVP and to the claim that building a model from scratch is not obviously needed.
3. Headline numbers sometimes hide seed variance or selection. R-012 reports best-of-three seeds as its headline; R-006's headline depends on a window setting that appears tuned on the reporting split.
4. For rPPG: R-005 and R-007 report high in-dataset scores on 2019 and 2020 data. R-008 reports that a pulse is present in high-quality face swaps and follows the driving video. R-009 reports, in a reproduction by other authors, a large drop for an older rPPG detector on talking-face fakes, with strong variation across generators. R-010 shows pulse signals can be forged on purpose. Together these motivate the feasibility test in D-006 rather than adoption.
5. Audio-visual: the largest labelled multimodal set found so far, FakeAVCeleb (R-014), has only 500 real videos, and naive fusion did poorly in the dataset paper.
6. Audio standalone versus out of corpus. The best in-corpus EER reported in the audio papers is about 1.2 percent (ASVspoof 2019 LA evaluation set, see R-012 and R-028); the In-the-Wild paper (R-033) reports out-of-corpus EERs for the models it tests far above that, about 33.9 percent for RawNet2 (a different model from the in-corpus best). On ASVspoof 2021 the EER of a team fell from 11.6 percent to 0.10 percent over the progress phase and was 15.6 percent in the evaluation phase (R-030, as summarised by stream C1). A wav2vec 2.0 front end lowered EER from 11.47 to 6.15 percent, best seed, train ASVspoof 2019 LA and test ASVspoof 2021 LA, with nothing else changed (R-029, verified in the paper text).
7. Fusion and a missing modality. Under in-dataset protocols, joint late fusion was no better than independent audio and video detectors combined by an OR rule (R-020: whole-sequence accuracy 94.25 versus 95.83 on FF++ and 89.15 versus 89.36 on DFDC). In LAV-DF a visual-only variant scored 58.55 average precision at IoU 0.5 against 76.90 for the multimodal model on the full test set, and 83.55 against 85.20 once audio-only fakes were removed (R-024, in-dataset, verified in the paper text). Stream C1 found no paper that handles a missing modality inside the model (AVFF needs both, R-023).
8. Frame-to-video aggregation is a protocol choice. For the same Xception model the video AUC was 61.44 with the mean of frame scores and 68.81 with the mean of the five highest (R-025, AV-Deepfake1M protocol, verified in the paper text).
9. Threshold selection. When the threshold is tuned on the test distribution ("oracle") accuracy is far above what a threshold chosen on training data gives: StyleGAN 87.1 versus 96.8 and DeepFake 53.5 versus 80.7 percent, classifiers trained on ProGAN (R-047, verified in the paper text; the extracted table has scrambled row labels and the assignment follows the caption order uncalibrated, two-shot, oracle).
10. Shortcuts in generated-image data. R-046 reports that file format and JPEG quality alone let detectors separate real from generated images in common benchmarks, so a leakage check should include format, JPEG quality and resolution as well as identity (stream C2; only the first number of its table was re-checked). R-044 reports that binary face-forgery detectors learn identity cues; it does not test whether FF++ train and test share identities.
11. Fine-tuning recipes. Under the protocol of R-035 (trained on ProGAN, mean accuracy at threshold 0.5), a linear probe scored 81.02, full fine-tuning 86.22, low-rank variants 91.06 to 93.53 and SVD-residual variants up to 95.19 (the ends of the range were verified in the paper text). Stream C2 reports that the rank was chosen on the same test sets used for the headline. The papers in this group used 24 GB GPUs where stated; no paper reports peak VRAM, so memory on a 12 GB card has to be measured here.
12. Seeds and intervals. Stream C2 found, by full-text search, no mention of seeds, standard deviations or confidence intervals in the papers it read on SBI, Effort, Forensics Adapter, NPR, GenImage, DIRE, the self-supervised ViT paper and Deepfake-Eval-2024 (R-034, R-035, R-048, R-039, R-037, R-040, R-050, R-049); the integrator did not repeat that search. The same method scores differently across papers (SBI on Celeb-DF v2: 93.18 in its own paper and 88.6 in the table of R-035, per stream C2). The Effort figure 0.830 quoted in R-009 cannot come from the Effort paper, which has no Celeb-DF++ experiment (stream C2).
13. rPPG and video compression. In lab video (stationary subject) pulse-rate error rose from 1.78 bpm raw to 3.35 bpm at constant rate factor 18 (x264), and with head motion from 1.18 to 8.66 bpm (R-059, verified in the paper text). Stream C3 reports that no paper read gives the bit rate of FF++, Celeb-DF or DFDC and that mapping FF++ c23 and c40 to that scale is an assumption; a cheap check of bit rates and pulse signal quality on real videos is the missing evidence for O-010.
14. rPPG on constructed and on current fakes. In a constructed set where fakes were made from a fixed real set (UBFC and COHFACE), the heart-rate error was 3.13 bpm for real, 5.70 bpm for face swap and 12.51 bpm for motion transfer, and an rPPG-only detector scored AUC 89.27 (face swap) and 95.29 (motion transfer) in-dataset with subject-disjoint 5-fold (R-065, verified in the paper text). On the Celeb-DF++ talking-face subset an rPPG-derived 1D ResNet ranged from AUC 0.698 to 0.988 across generators under an identity-disjoint split with five seeds (R-064, verified); that paper says its signal is not shown to be cardiac and that frame rate and window count differ between real and fake videos (stream C3; confounds that the negative controls of O-010 can target). DeepRhythm trained on FF++ reached 0.641 accuracy on DFDC-preview (R-054, verified); its in-dataset figure is reported much higher. R-067 is a single-author preprint and no number from it is used without replication.
15. Dataset difficulty differs. The same Xception, trained and tested within its own dataset, scored AUC 83.58 on LAV-DF and 68.68 on AV-Deepfake1M, so in-dataset numbers from different datasets are not comparable (R-024, R-025, stream C1; not re-checked).
