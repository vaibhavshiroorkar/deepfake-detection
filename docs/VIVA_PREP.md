## 8. Standard definitions (background, not experiment-dependent)

Notation: n test examples with labels y_i in {0, 1} (1 = fake) and model scores s_i in [0, 1] for the fake class. Let n_1 be the number of fakes and n_0 the number of real examples.

- ROC-AUC. The probability that a randomly chosen fake receives a higher score than a randomly chosen real example, counting ties as one half: AUC = (1 / (n_1 n_0)) * sum over fake i and real j of [ 1(s_i > s_j) + 0.5 * 1(s_i = s_j) ]. It equals the area under the curve of true positive rate against false positive rate as the threshold sweeps from 1 to 0. It uses ranking only, so it does not depend on calibration and is unchanged by any increasing transformation of the scores.
- Equal error rate (EER). The value of the false positive rate (equal to the false negative rate) at the threshold where those two rates are equal. It is read from the ROC curve and summarises ranking quality at one operating point. The threshold at which it occurs depends on the data it is computed on.
- Log loss. The mean of -[ y_i ln p_i + (1 - y_i) ln(1 - p_i) ], where p_i is the predicted probability of fake. A model that always says 0.5 scores ln 2, about 0.693; R-003 uses this as the no-information level.
- Brier score. The mean of (p_i - y_i)^2 over the test set. It is a proper scoring rule, so it is minimised in expectation only by reporting the true probabilities. The definition is standard; the original source (Brier, 1950) has not been read in this project.
- Expected calibration error (ECE), as defined in R-016. Divide the confidence range into M equal-width bins B_1, ..., B_M, where the confidence of an example is the predicted probability of its predicted class. Then ECE = sum over m of (|B_m| / n) * | acc(B_m) - conf(B_m) |, where acc is the fraction of correct predictions in the bin and conf is the mean confidence. R-016 uses M = 15. ECE depends on M and on how the bins are formed.
- Temperature scaling, as in R-016. Replace the logits z by z / T with a single scalar T > 0 chosen to minimise the negative log likelihood on a validation set. It does not change which class is predicted, so accuracy and AUC are unchanged; only the confidence changes.
- Bootstrap confidence interval. Resample the test units (identities or source videos, not frames) with replacement B times; recompute the metric on each resample; the interval is formed from the lower and upper quantiles of the B values, for example the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles for a 95 percent interval. A paired comparison of two models uses the same resampled units for both and takes the interval of the difference of their metrics. B and the level are fixed in Stage 2.
- Minimum tandem detection cost function (min t-DCF), used in audio anti-spoofing (R-012, R-013). It measures the cost of a detector when it is used together with a speaker verification system, so it combines the detector's errors with the verification system's. It is not used for visual detection.

## Cross-questions on the iteration results (added 2026-10-25, evidence: EXPERIMENTS.md E-005 to E-013, DECISIONS.md D-051 to D-061)

- Q: Why did fine-tuning help so much (video task, dev_x 0.7396 to 0.9096)? A: The probe of IMG-17 was already underfit on the training data (train AUC 0.9245 on the strided subset); the fine-tuned class contains the probe class (MATH_NOTES 11), so the training AUC can only rise, and here it rose to 0.9994 and the dev_x AUC rose with it (paired difference +0.1700 [+0.1228, +0.2114], E-005). The Xception baseline fine-tuned with the same recipe reaches dev_x 0.8750 [0.8435, 0.9089] (E-013), so most of the gain comes from fine-tuning itself; IMG-17 minus Xception is +0.0346 [+0.0136, +0.0550] on dev_x, exploratory. The dev_x slice has 15 identity groups, so none of this is population-level.
- Q: Why only one learning rate of three worked? A: At 3e-5 and 1e-4 most encoders collapsed to near chance (validation 0.52 to 0.71); the grid was fixed before the run (D-042) and the best rate was the smallest for every encoder, which means the grid may not contain a better smaller rate; this is stated as a limit. For Xception the selected rate 1e-4 is the largest of the grid, also an edge.
- Q: Is the speech result a deepfake-speech detector? A: Not outside ASVspoof in a way that is shown: dev_x is In-the-Wild with 12 speakers; after trimming (+0.0623), augmentation (+0.0732) and the three-encoder average (+0.0675) the dev_x AUC is 0.8130 [0.7523, 0.8808] against 0.6100 for the first working version. The intervals are wide, a gain below about +0.09 cannot be shown with 12 speakers, and RMS energy still separates the classes on In-the-Wild with AUC 0.135, so a level-related cue remains. All of it is exploratory.
- Q: Why not claim audio-visual detection from the fused LAV-DF AUC of 0.9935? A: The control of D-059: within the videos that went through the dataset's own pipeline (video fake, both fake and audio-only fake), the in-domain visual head has AUC 0.5070 [0.4771, 0.5361] against the visual label, and it separates the real originals from the audio-only fakes (whose frames are untouched) with 0.9912. The head learned "original or produced". The audio head within produced videos has 0.8574 against the audio label, so it is a real audio-manipulation detector on this dataset. A visual head trained on produced videos only also stayed at 0.5169 (E-012). The control was run after seeing E-011 and is labelled post-hoc.
- Q: Why does the FF++-trained visual head have AUC 0.38 on LAV-DF, below chance? A: It also scores the audio-only fakes (video untouched) as more real than the real originals (AUC 0.29 for that cell), so it responds to something other than the manipulation, probably an encoding or pipeline property that is aligned differently in FF++ and in LAV-DF. A sign reversal of this kind is not a small shift, and fitting a negative weight to it (the E-010 regression reached 0.75) would not hold on another dataset, so it was rejected as a method (D-058).
- Q: Why was rPPG dropped? A: The pre-declared sanity check failed: 130 of 432 real clips (0.301 [0.260, 0.346]) had a pulse-band SNR above the 95th percentile of the time-shuffled control, against the rule of at least 0.6; per dataset 0.280, 0.288, 0.333. The statement is "rPPG signal not recoverable from these data", not "no pulse exists": the check has no pulse ground truth and the videos are compressed (D-052).
- Q: How is "comparable to published detectors" decided, and what is delta? A: Non-inferiority (D-025): the lower end of the paired interval of (ours minus the re-run baseline) must be above minus delta, with delta fixed before the sealed run from the seed spread of the baseline (D-060). First pass: delta 0.0143 at learning rate 1e-4 (the edge of the grid). On the user's review the grid was extended to 3e-4 (E-013b, selected on validation); the seed spread fell, so delta is the floor 0.0100 (D-066). The baseline is Xception fine-tuned with the same recipe on the same data. The sealed comparison is not run yet; it waits for the user's go-ahead.
- Q: Why did one run need a repeat? A: The IMG-08 run at 1e-4 stopped at about step 1,750 on a data-loader worker error; it was repeated once with the identical configuration and seed, and the error record is kept (E-005 log note).


- Q: Why three confirmatory tests and what is the 98.33 percent line? A: The tests are T1 video final system against I-0, T2 speech final system against I-0, T3 final system against the baseline (D-062 b). Three tests raise the chance of one false positive; a Bonferroni level of 1 - 0.05 / 3 = 98.33 percent is printed from the same bootstrap replicates as a robustness line. The declared 95 percent verdicts are not changed by it; a verdict the 98.33 percent interval does not support is called not robust to the Bonferroni level.
- Q: You ran two more video iterations, why did neither enter? A: I-1b (augmentation and mixup) raised dev_x by 0.022 [0.009, 0.038] but lowered in-dataset validation AUC by 0.019, more than the allowance of 0.0079 written before the run (E-016, D-065): a trade-off, and the rule put in-dataset loss first. I-1c (mean of three encoders) gave +0.010 [-0.002, +0.023] on dev_x, not distinguishable from zero, as the variance formula predicted for score correlations of 0.89 to 0.91 (E-017). Both triggers of ITERATIONS.md were not met to the letter; they were run on request and reported as such.
- Q: The pulse check failed; how do you know the pipeline can see a pulse at all? A: Positive control M-005: a known 1.2 Hz sinusoid injected into the skin pixels of the same 432 clips is recovered in 87 percent of them at a relative green amplitude of 0.008 and in 55 percent at 0.004, and the control reproduces the S-1 number. This shows sensitivity to a clean sinusoid of that size, not that a real pulse of that size exists in compressed video, and the S-1 verdict is unchanged.

- Q: What did the sealed test show? A: One evaluation per frozen iteration (E-014, ledger of 74 entries). Video on the sealed Celeb-DF family: final system 0.8218 against 0.6238 for the working version (+0.1980 [+0.1688, +0.2242]); speech on the sealed In-the-Wild test: 0.9148 against 0.7516 (+0.1633 [+0.1177, +0.2266]); both survive the Bonferroni level. Against the Xception baseline the video system is +0.0176 [+0.0025, +0.0327]: superior by the declared 95 percent rule, but the 98.33 percent interval [-0.0011, +0.0355] includes zero, so only non-inferiority (margin 0.0100) is robust. Two speech predictions failed upward, and the video AUC is lower on the sealed set than on the development slice (0.8218 against 0.9096), so the development numbers were optimistic for video and pessimistic for speech.
- Q: Why can the family set not simply be read as 7,650 independent videos? A: The interval is governed by 344 identity groups, and the set mixes Celeb-DF v2 test videos and Celeb-DF++ fakes scored against the Celeb-DF v2 reals (Celeb-DF++ has no reals of its own, so on its own it has no AUC).


- Q: Your dashboard "explains" results. Is this explainable AI, and how do you know an explanation is not invented? A: It is a faithful description of each module's stored result, not an explanation of a network's internal reasoning. Every sentence is generated by a fixed template from named fields of that result (D-076, src/dfd/platform/explain.py) and lists them; a validator rejects any sentence whose numbers are not in its cited fields, any cited field that does not exist, model-output sentences on a module that did not run, wording such as "authentic" or "proves", and an experimental score shown as a headline number. 38 tests check this, including tampered explanations that must be caught. No language model writes text.
- Q: Why no overall fake probability? A: The modules measure different things on different evidence (faces, speech, timing, metadata), with different and partly exploratory calibrations; a combined probability would need a joint model of their errors that nothing in this project has measured. The summary therefore only counts checks per group and states that no overall verdict is given.
- Q: The generated-video model scored 1.00 on a real clip in your demo. Why show it at all? A: It is shown as an experimental signal, in its own group, never as a finding (the gate APPROVED_HEADS is empty). E-026 measured why: on 440 real clips from FF++ and LAV-DF, 338 scored above its threshold (0.7682 [0.7265, 0.8052]) against 19 of 100 DeepAction real clips out of fold, so the head mostly separates DeepAction's stock-footage source from everything else. Together with the chance-level DF26 result (E-020) this is the evidence that it is not a detector.
- Q: What does the lip-sync curve on the screen show? A: The mean SyncNet embedding distance at each of the 31 candidate audio-visual offsets that the module already computed (D5); the lowest point is the chosen offset and the shaded band (-6 to +2 frames) is the range fitted on real clips in E-025. It shows how the timing result was read; it is not a manipulation score.
- Q: Does an AI-generated portrait get checked by your generated-image detector? A: (Updated 2026-10-27, D-083.) The image analysis itself still sends a still image with a face only to the face-swap model built for video frames (routing unchanged, UX-7). A separate experimental card now also gives the whole image to the frozen generated-image model through the adapter's own whole-image function and shows its score, but makes no finding, because that model was not validated on images with a face; the measurement on faces is E-028. (Earlier answer, kept as history: No. A still image with a detectable face goes only to the face-swap model built for video frames; the generated-image model runs only when no face is found (UX-7). The report says so on every such result. Changing the routing is a detector change (D8), deferred until a modern-generator and portrait measurement exists (roadmap section 19).)

- Q: Your generated-video head was at chance on DF26. Did you fix it? A: Partly, and we measured what is still wrong (E-027, preregistered). Trained only on DF26's six open generators, as its licence allows, with real clips split by source video and whole generator families held out, a CLIP ViT-L/14 frame-average head reached 0.7453 [0.7174, 0.7710] on unseen families and 0.7548 [0.7189, 0.7885] in one counted look at Grok Imagine, Kling 3.0, Veo 3.1 and Wan 2.6 (Kling hardest, 0.6483). But at its balanced threshold it flags 80 percent of unmodified FF++ and LAV-DF real clips, so it still partly detects the kind of real footage, not generation; that is why it is not installed (D-085).
- Q: Does the video head use motion? A: No evidence that it does. Temporal statistics over 14 frames were worse than the plain average (-0.0404 [-0.0650, -0.0165]), and shuffling frame order changed nothing (-0.0029 [-0.0148, 0.0095]). It compares appearance.
- Q: Is your speech detector better than AASIST? A: No. On the same official ASVspoof 2019 LA evaluation set with the same training data, our EER is 6.94 percent against AASIST's 1.13 percent (E-030, computed afterwards from the stored sealed scores, no model run). Our design choice was transfer: on the 42 sealed In-the-Wild speakers our EER is 15.8 percent; published full-set values are 33.9 (Muller et al.) and 7.2 percent (Pascu et al., a 2-billion-parameter encoder). Different speaker sets, so we state the numbers and do not compute a gap.
- Q: Why do you call most literature comparisons only 'partially comparable'? A: Because a difference is meaningful only when task, test data, protocol and metric all match. Image papers report average precision, speech papers EER, many video papers use the official Celeb-DF test list, which we replaced by an identity-separated split (D-035). docs/PERFORMANCE_BENCHMARK.md classifies every row and computes differences only for the directly comparable ones.

## Cross-questions added 2026-10-27 (evening): modern images, held-out protection, product scope (evidence: EXPERIMENTS.md E-027 to E-030, DECISIONS.md D-083 to D-086)

- Q: Why does the generated-video head (E-027) stay out of the product although its AUC on the four commercial generators is 0.7548? A: Because the AUC says it ranks DF26's own clips well, not that it detects generation in general. At its balanced threshold it flags 353 of 440 (0.8023 [0.7625, 0.8368]) unmodified real clips from two other sources (FF++ originals 0.75, LAV-DF 0.8267), against 0.2841 of DF26's own real clips. A product that calls more than a quarter of the real clips of its own data, and about four fifths of real clips from other sources, "generated-like" would be wrong in the way that matters most. The real side of the commercial look was also reused, and it was the second look at that subset. The preregistered success line was met and is reported as met; the product decision (D-085) rests on the confound, not on the AUC. APPROVED_HEADS stays empty.
- Q: What does "80 percent of real videos flagged" mean exactly? A: At the threshold that was best on DF26's out-of-fold scores, 105 of 140 FaceForensics++ originals and 248 of 300 LAV-DF real clips scored above it. It means the head responds to the kind of footage (source, framing, compression, content), not only to generation, so the score cannot be read as evidence of generation. It does not mean 80 percent of all real videos everywhere; it is measured on these 440 clips and the pooled interval is [0.7625, 0.8368].
- Q: How do E-028 and E-029 differ? A: E-028 measures the installed generated-image head exactly as shipped, trained on ProGAN images, with nothing fitted: macro AUC 0.7039 [0.6855, 0.7229] over 21 modern generators, and it misses 74 percent of the generated images at its threshold (inadequate by the line fixed in advance). E-029 asks whether training data is the reason: the same kind of head (frozen CLIP features, linear layer) retrained on 125 recent generators, three seeds, then scored once on the same images. Ranking improves to 0.8839 (+0.1800 [+0.1643, +0.1959]), but its validation threshold flags 52 percent of real images, so it is not usable and not installed. Together: more diverse training data fixes ranking, not calibration to real sources the head has not seen.
- Q: How was the held-out evaluation protected from tuning? A: (1) The evaluation images come from a different dataset release than the training images, no generator name is shared, no real image name is shared, and the split is by generator and by real source (and by device for VISION), written to a file before any feature was read. (2) The preregistration (hypothesis, success and failure lines, split rule, encoder rule) was hashed before extraction, and I re-derived the hash from the document: it matches. (3) Fitting, early stopping, temperature, threshold and encoder choice use validation images only. (4) The heads were frozen and hashed (freeze.json) before the evaluation script ran, and the script refuses to run without --final, after a hash mismatch or a second time. (5) One look. Two things to be candid about: a dry run on 7 shards and a code test with random heads were done beforehand, disclosed in the log, and used for nothing; and the encoder that validation ranked lower scored higher on the evaluation images, but the rule was applied as written. No threshold was chosen on the evaluation images, because that would be tuning on the test set.
- Q: Why compare the speech system with AASIST by EER, and why only partially on In-the-Wild? A: Published speech detectors report equal error rate, and E-014 had reported AUC, so no comparison was possible. EER was computed afterwards from the stored sealed scores (E-030, D-084): no model was rerun, nothing was selected or tuned, the recomputed AUCs equal the frozen ones, and the score file hash equals its provenance record. On ASVspoof 2019 LA with the official training data our EER is 6.94 percent [5.95, 7.82] against 1.13 percent for AASIST, so the published system is better by 5.81 points. On In-the-Wild our 15.8 percent [12.7, 18.8] is on 42 sealed speakers; the published values (33.9 and 7.2 percent) are on the full set of 54, so no gap is computed.
- Q: What are the limitations of the whole-image check on images with a face (D-083)? A: It is the frozen ProGAN-trained head applied to the whole image; E-028 measured it on 3,119 face-containing images: AUC 0.7859 [0.7717, 0.7995], but it misses 80.6 percent of generated faces at its threshold. It is better than the face-swap path on the same images (0.6020; paired +0.1839 [+0.1596, +0.2074]), yet neither is usable. So it is shown as an experimental card, makes no finding, has no decision line, and its text says most generated faces were missed. A single demo image (score 0.02) says nothing about reliability, and a low score does not show that an image is a photograph.
- Q: Which modules are approved and which are experimental? A: Findings come from the face-video, image (face-swap on a face crop, generated-image detector when no face is found) and speech detectors and from file and Content Credentials checks, each with its stated tested scope; the three frozen detectors were evaluated on sealed tests. Experimental, never a finding: the generated-video module (APPROVED_HEADS is empty), the whole-image check for images with a face, and the lip-sync module is weak evidence with a band of -6 to +2 frames. No module gives an overall score or says media is authentic. The E-027 and E-029 heads are research artefacts and are not installed.
- Q: What evidence supports the major claims? A: The sealed staircase (E-014) for the frozen video and speech systems, with group bootstrap intervals and the non-inferiority rule; E-015 for FakeAVCeleb; E-018 to E-027 for generated video, each negative or limited result kept in the log; E-028 and E-029 for modern images; E-030 for the speech EER. Every number in the paper, report and slides is traced to a logged experiment, and for E-027, E-028, E-029 and E-030 the numbers were recomputed independently from stored scores.
- Q: What are the most important limitations and what would you do next? A: Limits: the video and speech results rest on 344 groups and 42 speakers; generated-video detection does not transfer across sources (confound); the modern-image evaluation is a cost-selected subset with format differences between real and generated images; thresholds do not transfer to new real sources; nothing was tested on edited or socially shared media. Next, in order: a threshold calibrated on diverse real sources for the retrained image head with a false-positive line on sources it was not calibrated on; real clips from many sources for generated-video training; full-scale Community Forensics training.

## Cross-questions added 2026-10-28: per-module verdicts and the Evaluation Lab (evidence: DECISIONS.md D-087, D-088; EXPERIMENTS.md E-014, E-015, E-028)

- Q: Why does the report give a verdict per check but no overall verdict? A: The checks look at different evidence (face frames, whole image, speech, lip sync, provenance) and their scores were calibrated on different validation sets; an overall statement would need a joint error model of the checks, and nothing in this project measured one (D-087). The schema keeps refusing report-level keys such as `verdict` and `overall_score` (`FORBIDDEN_REPORT_KEYS`, unchanged since HEAD 5af239a), and the report keeps only counts per group.
- Q: Why does the face-video flag read "Possible manipulation" without "(weak evidence)", while the image and speech flags carry it? A: The wording follows the measured error rates on sealed data. On the sealed FakeAVCeleb half the frozen video threshold flagged 0.4 percent of real videos (specificity 0.996) but caught only 48 percent of fakes (E-015), so a flag is rarely a false alarm and a non-flag is weak; hence "No strong evidence of manipulation detected" with high uncertainty. The image and speech checks have no comparable sealed evidence for their real-world use (E-028: the whole-image head misses 74 percent of modern generated images; E-015: audio specificity 0.6367), so their flags are "weak evidence" and their non-flags are "Inconclusive" or "No strong evidence".
- Q: Are the scores probabilities? A: No. A temperature was fitted on each detector's validation split, so the score behaves like a probability only for data like that split; E-015 and E-028 show it does not on other sources. The platform text was corrected to say so (D-087); the frozen v1 page keeps its older wording because that path is protected, and this inconsistency is recorded.
- Q: Can an experimental module ever produce a finding? A: No. Experimental modules always get "Experimental signal observed, no reliable verdict available", whatever the score; "Likely AI-generated" exists in the vocabulary, but no rule reaches it, and a unit test pins this. `APPROVED_HEADS` is empty.
- Q: Could the Evaluation Lab be used to tune on the test set? A: It is built to prevent that (D-088). It runs no model and fits nothing on the evaluated rows: the threshold comes from the validation rows of the same runs or is given with its provenance. A sealed split runs only as a re-analysis of an evaluation already in the ledger, only with purpose "final_reanalysis", and only if the score file's SHA-256 equals the hash recorded in the ledger. The lab writes no ledger entry. It also refuses a sealed run when a group appears in both the evaluated block and the train or validation rows. Checked on 2026-10-28 on the real stored scores: a re-analysis of I-4 on the Celeb-DF family reproduced the E-014 AUC point estimate 0.8218, the ledger bytes were unchanged, and a first look under purpose "development" was refused (docs/TESTING_RECORD.md section 10).
- Q: Does a good Evaluation Lab result approve a model for the product? A: No. The lab never reads or writes `APPROVED_HEADS`, thresholds or checkpoints; approval stays a recorded decision in DECISIONS.md backed by a preregistered experiment.
- Q: The face-video flag rests on specificity 0.996 on FakeAVCeleb. Is the threshold that specific everywhere? A: No, and the paper and report say so; this is why the flag reads "Possible manipulation" and not "Likely manipulated". On the other sealed video sets the false-alarm rate at the frozen threshold is higher: a post hoc computation from the stored sealed scores (docs/PERFORMANCE_BENCHMARK.md sections 12.4 and 13.8; not an experiment entry, no model run) gives a seed-mean false-positive rate of about 0.11 on the Celeb-DF family and 0.14 on DFD. The user decided on 2026-10-28 to replace "Likely manipulated" by "Possible manipulation", with high uncertainty (D-087 addendum). The threshold itself is frozen and was not changed, and the wording does not claim that the check is reliable.
