# C2: literature stream C2 (generalising visual detectors, fine-tuning, generated-image benchmarks, evaluation methodology, calibration)

Author of this note: Stream C2 agent. Date: 2026-10-24. Scope: this file only; nothing was committed, no shared document was edited. IDs below are temporary (C2-01 to C2-17) and are to be renumbered into docs/RESEARCH.md by the integrator.

How the papers were obtained and read: each PDF was downloaded with curl from arxiv.org/pdf/<ID> into a scratch folder outside the repository (scratchpad\streamC2\pdf), converted with pdftotext (layout mode and plain mode), and read from the converted text. arXiv IDs were found by an arXiv API title query and then each ID was checked against the title page of the downloaded file (title and authors match in all 17 cases; nothing was taken from memory). Where a venue is not printed in the file, the row says so. Statements about "no seeds / no intervals" were checked by searching the whole extracted text of each paper for the words seed, standard deviation, confidence interval, bootstrap. Tables were read from text extraction; where the extraction scrambled a table, the row says which numbers were not used.

Status vocabulary: READ, PARTIAL (sections listed), NR, TO READ. Rule for numbers: each result names its protocol; in-dataset and cross-dataset/cross-generator numbers are never in one cell. All numbers are the papers' numbers, not ours.

## (b) Findings that bear on open decisions

Every number carries its protocol. These are evidence and implications, not choices.

### O-004 Split and leakage policy

1. Shortcut features other than identity can leak through a split. In GenImage (C2-13), a ResNet-50 trained on the Midjourney subset called natural FFHQ 1024 px images natural 80.45 percent of the time as PNG and 100 percent of the time after JPEG quality 60 compression (accuracy on natural images only; same detector, same images, only the file encoding differs). After constraining both classes to JPEG 96 and similar sizes, mean cross-generator accuracy rose from 71.68 to 82.74 (ResNet-50) and from 74.09 to 85.83 (Swin-T) (cross-generator, GenImage subsets SD1.5, SD1.4, Wukong as training sets). Implication: the leakage check script (still unwritten) should test class-vs-split balance of container format, JPEG quality, resolution and aspect ratio, not only identity and source video. For FF++ this includes compression level (c23 vs c40, R-001) and for audio sampling rate, duration and codec, which I did not find evidence on in this stream.
2. Identity is learned from binary labels alone. In C2-11, detectors trained on FF++ carry linearly decodable identity information, and the identity-interaction score alone has AUC 81.53 to 99.58 percent on FF++ test but 44.43 to 49.47 percent on Celeb-DF (FF++-trained models; in-dataset and cross-dataset in separate columns). The paper does not test whether FF++'s fixed train/test videos share identities, so it supports an identity-disjoint split as a precaution (option d of O-004) but does not prove overlap in any given split.
3. Selection of the checkpoint without any fake image is possible (C2-01: validation on real videos and their SBIs), which removes one route by which a validation split can leak generator knowledge into model selection.
4. The in-the-wild fine-tuning protocol in C2-16 (60 percent train, 40 percent test) does not state whether the split is by source; I would not copy it as a template.
5. Duplicates: C2-16 removed duplicates by hash plus manual review including near-duplicates (different crops). That is a worked example for the near-duplicate item in O-004's criteria.
6. Generator-disjoint test sets are standard in the generated-image line: C2-05 holds out 21 generators (26K images), C2-04 trains on one generator and tests on all eight, and its same-generator accuracy (at least 98.8 percent) versus cross-generator (52.0 to 99.9 for ResNet-50 trained on SD 1.4, accuracy over the eight test subsets) shows the gap that a random split would hide.

### O-005 Metrics, threshold policy, calibration

1. Threshold cost is large cross-generator. C2-14 (train ProGAN, ResNet-50 with blur+JPEG at 10 percent, accuracy): uncalibrated 87.1, oracle (threshold picked on the test set) 96.8 for StyleGAN; DeepFake 53.5 versus 80.7; SAN 50.5 versus 68.0. A threshold set on test data gives numbers that cannot be reached in practice, which is the same warning already recorded for R-011. A threshold set on validation data from the training distribution sits at or near the "uncalibrated" column.
2. Accuracy and AUC disagree on trends. C2-12 (CLIP + SVM, generators unseen): AUC rises with the number of reference pairs N while accuracy at threshold 0.5 does not, because the optimal threshold moves away from 0.5. C2-05 notes a wider accuracy gap than mAP gap between a diverse and a narrow training set and attributes it to threshold calibration (the authors' interpretation, not tested separately).
3. Report-metric practice: C2-16 argues against reporting one metric and reports AUC, F1 and accuracy together (EER for audio in supplementary); on in-the-wild data open models sit at AUC near 0.5 (maximum 0.58), a floor effect that makes metric choice nearly irrelevant to ranking at that point.
4. Calibration metric: ECE as used in R-016 depends on the number and placement of bins (C2-08: ECE of histogram binning is one third of its class-conditional ECE on CIFAR-10; adaptive-binning rank correlation across bin counts 0.5927 versus 0.3677 for equal-width) and on the data size (C2-09: ImageNet VGG16 after recalibration looks like about 0.02 at 15 bins and at least twice that at finer bins). Implication: define ECE in the document (bin count, equal-width or adaptive, norm), report the bin count, and report a proper score (Brier or log loss, as in C2-10's recipe and as the DFDC organisers used log loss in R-003) alongside it. No detector paper in this stream reports any of these, so there is no published number to compare to.
5. Seeds as ensemble members (C2-10): the comparison rules already require at least 3 seeds per candidate; deep-ensemble predictions across those seeds give an uncertainty estimate and often better scoring-rule values than one model, at no training beyond the rules. Cost axes would add inference time of M models. This is an option to evaluate, not a decision; the paper's default is M = 5.
6. Operating thresholds in C2-17: thresholds were EER-based from validation sets; for the unseen diffusion set it recalibrated the threshold on unseen-domain validation data. That breaks the "validation from the training distribution only" practice and should be labelled if copied.

### O-006 MVP design

1. Frozen features with a linear/SVM head are cheap and, for fully generated images, strong: C2-12 reaches high AUC with 1k+1k training pairs (cross-generator, 18 generators); R-011 gave the same pattern. For face video the evidence in this stream is weaker: C2-17 (frozen last-4-block features, seen set, EER percent) gives 11.98 for DINOv2 and 16.51 for EVA-02-CLIP, against 5.63 for fine-tuned DINOv2; so a frozen-feature MVP is a valid first baseline but is expected to be beaten by partial fine-tuning under that protocol.
2. A frozen backbone is not always the best end state. C2-05 found freezing the backbone consistently worse than end-to-end training when 2.7M diverse images were available (figure only, no number read), and C2-02 measured full fine-tuning of CLIP at 86.22 mAcc, above linear probing at 81.02 mAcc and below the SVD method at 95.19 (train ProGAN, 19 generators, fixed threshold 0.5). So the ordering linear probe < full fine-tune < structured partial fine-tune appeared under C2-02's protocol, while C2-05's large-data protocol favoured end-to-end over frozen. The data amount and diversity change the ranking, which the MVP-to-iteration plan can test.
3. The MVP must run end to end under the Stage 2 protocol; every dataset in this stream has format shortcuts (C2-13), so a high MVP number on a single dataset should be treated as unvalidated until the leakage check exists.

### O-007 Candidate backbones

1. Capacity: C2-01 Table 7 (train FF++ real-only SBIs, mean video-level AUC over FF++/CDF/DFDCP/FFIW): EfficientNet-b4 90.95 > EfficientNet-b1 88.77 > ResNet-50 87.65 ~ ResNet-152 87.62 > Xception 86.28; single runs; the authors say larger networks tend to generalise better. Spread across seeds is unknown, so differences below about 1 point cannot be called.
2. Transformer versus conv: C2-04 (train SD 1.4, accuracy over 8 generators) ResNet-50 72.1, DeiT-S 71.6, Swin-T 74.8; C2-05 found ViT-S (CLIP-pretrained) and ConvNeXt-S similar across three training sets. C2-17 (frozen last-4 features, seen EER): self-supervised ViTs 11.9 to 12.0 against ConvNet 21.71; fine-tuned: 5.63 to 6.88 versus EfficientNetV2 15.05. Note the unequal k budgets there.
3. Parameter counts of reference detectors: NPR 1.44M parameters (C2-06), Forensics Adapter 5.7M trainable on a 307M frozen ViT-L (C2-15), Effort 0.19M trainable (C2-02), SBI EfficientNet-B4 about 18M (as listed in C2-02), others 19M to 133M (C2-02 Table 1). Trainable parameters are not memory: the frozen ViT-L still runs forward and backward, and no paper here reports VRAM.
4. Audio: nothing new beyond C2-16, where AASIST, RawNet2 and P3 off-the-shelf on 2024 in-the-wild audio gave near-chance AUC; relevant to scoping claims for the audio branch (R-012, R-013).

### O-008 Fine-tuning strategy

1. Rank of evidence (all under each paper's own protocol, no seeds): C2-02 (train ProGAN, 19 generator subsets, accuracy at 0.5, mAcc): linear probe 81.02 < full fine-tune 86.22 < LoRA variants 91.06 to 93.53 < SVD residual variants 92.13 to 95.19. C2-17 (face images, seen-set EER): frozen head about 12 < one block fine-tuned about 11.3 < about half of the blocks fine-tuned 5.63; too many blocks risks overfitting on a small set (authors).
2. The rationale offered by C2-02: full fine-tuning and LoRA reduced the effective rank of the feature space (PCA, general domain: 479 before, 304 LoRA, 238 full fine-tune, 316 SVD method) and the detector overfits to the few fake patterns. This is a mechanism claim of the authors, tested only by their own diagnostics.
3. Hyperparameter selection caution: C2-02 chose its rank from ablations on the same test sets used for its headline; C2-17's k-ablation figure does not name its split. Under our rules both would need a validation-only sweep.
4. Compute: C2-03's memory saving comes from not storing optimiser states for frozen weights (up to 2/3 of VRAM for Adam on GPT-3, an NLP figure). On a vision ViT-L the frozen weights' activations are still stored for the backward pass; so the saving on 12 GB must be measured, not assumed. C2-15 and C2-06 used a 24 GB RTX 3090; C2-05 trained with batch 512 and mixed precision; none reports peak VRAM or seconds per epoch. These are the cost axes DECISIONS requires and they will have to be measured here.
5. Recipe values on record that fit small batches: C2-15 (Adam lr 2e-4, weight decay 5e-4, batch 16, frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 at 224, adapter at 256), C2-02 (Adam lr 2e-4 fixed, batch 32), C2-05 (AdamW lr 2e-5, batch 512, for a full ViT-S), C2-14 (Adam lr 1e-4, batch 64, full ResNet-50). Full-model and adapter learning rates differ by a factor of ten in these papers, consistent with a learning-rate sensitivity axis worth measuring.
