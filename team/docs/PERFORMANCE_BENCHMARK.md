## 1. Rules of this file

1. Our numbers are copied exactly from docs/EXPERIMENTS.md (experiment ID, split and interval given with each). Sealed results are E-014 (staircase and confirmatory tests T1 to T3) and E-015 (FakeAVCeleb sealed identity half). Everything on a development slice (dev_x), on validation data, or on DeepAction is exploratory and labelled so. Since 2026-10-27 the file also contains a few values computed from stored score files (marked with a dagger or a double dagger in section 10 and listed with script, input hash and output in section 12.4); they are labelled "computed 2026-10-27" and were not in EXPERIMENTS.md.
2. Published numbers come only from papers in docs/RESEARCH.md (READ, or PARTIAL for the parts read). Each names its table or section. "(re-checked 2026-10-27)" means the number was read again on that day in the paper's own text (pdftotext of the arXiv or proceedings PDF, or the arXiv or ar5iv HTML rendering); "(RESEARCH.md row)" means it is taken from the matrix row that an earlier session filled from the paper.
3. Comparability categories (a letter on every row of section 3 and section 10). The earlier three classes are kept in brackets for traceability.
   - **A, Directly comparable** [Directly comparable]: same dataset release and the same test items (or the benchmark's own official split), same task and label definition, same metric and aggregation level, and the same declared training protocol. Any remaining difference (for example the published training set also contains the dev part, or the clip length differs) is stated in the row and is not expected to change the ordering by itself. Only A rows get a numerical difference (ours minus theirs, in the unit of the metric).
   - **B, Same dataset, different protocol** [Partially comparable]: the dataset is the same but at least one material condition differs: test subset or split, metric family (AUC against AP, EER or accuracy), aggregation level (frame against video), number of frames, training data or compression. No difference is computed; the row names the condition.
   - **C, Cross-dataset or out-of-distribution** [Context only, where the pairing crosses datasets]: one side is evaluated on data its training did not cover while the other side is in-dataset (for example our zero-shot FakeAVCeleb value against a published value trained and tested on FakeAVCeleb), or the two sides use different datasets for the same task. Shown to indicate the size of the in-dataset to out-of-dataset gap, not to rank. The LAV-DF row of section 3.1 is both B (our in-domain head against the in-dataset LAV-DF numbers) and C (the FF++-trained head).
   - **D, Contextual reference only** [Context only]: different task, dataset or setting. Orientation only.
   Mapping applied on 2026-10-27: Directly comparable becomes A; Partially comparable becomes B (every earlier Partially comparable row compared the same dataset under a different protocol; none was cross-dataset); Context only becomes D, or C where the only difference is that one side is in-dataset and the other zero-shot (the FakeAVCeleb rows). One row was upgraded from Partially comparable to A after a source check (Pascu et al. on ASVspoof 2019, section 12). A difference in training data alone does not demote a row from A when the published detectors' protocol is the benchmark's declared one and the row says so (DF26 zero-shot).
4. A larger number is never written as "better than". A difference is reported as a difference, with its interval when one exists. Published numbers come without intervals or seed counts unless stated, so even a directly comparable difference is not a significance test.
5. Placeholders: `[pending E-027]` and `[pending E-028/E-029]` marked rows whose result belonged to experiments that were running when this file was written; all of them were filled from docs/EXPERIMENTS.md by the main agent on 2026-10-27 (E-027 earlier, E-028 and E-029 in the evening, after independent recomputation of the stored scores). (Note, 2026-10-27 15:10: the E-027 RESULT block has since been appended to docs/EXPERIMENTS.md; its numbers were not copied here, by instruction.)
6. AUC is on a 0 to 1 scale for our results; many papers print percent. Differences are given in the paper's unit and in AUC points where they are computed.

## 4. Per-module analysis

### 4.1 face_video (T-V)

- Closest published benchmarks: Effort (0.956 Celeb-DF v2, 0.965 DFD, video AUC, R-035 Table 1), SBI (93.18 and 97.56, R-034 Table 9), Forensics Adapter (0.957 Celeb-DF v2 video, R-048), LipForensics (82.4 Celeb-DF v2, R-021 Table 2), DeepfakeBench frame-level Xception (0.7365 and 0.8163, R-017 Table 3).
- Our result: Celeb-DF v2 0.8999 [0.8759, 0.9210], DFD 0.8797 [0.8566, 0.9041], Celeb-DF family 0.8218 [0.8020, 0.8393] (E-014, sealed).
- Gap where comparable: against our own re-run Xception under the same protocol, +0.0176 [+0.0025, +0.0327] on the family (T3; not robust at the Bonferroni level), +0.0472 on Celeb-DF v2, -0.0050 on DFD, -0.0246 on Celeb-DF++ (point estimates). Against the published external detectors no difference is computed because the test subsets differ. On the 399 official-list Celeb-DF v2 videos that lie in our test_x the sealed scores give 0.8478 [0.7998, 0.8884] for I-1 and 0.7615 [0.7096, 0.8087] for the re-run Xception (computed 2026-10-27, post hoc, section 12); this is lower than the 0.956 of Effort in point estimate, a category B comparison.
- Likely reasons for the lower values than CLIP-based published detectors (hypotheses, not measured): a base-size encoder (SigLIP base, 93M) against CLIP ViT-L/14 (304M), which does not fit full fine-tuning on our 12 GB card (D-042); 1,800 optimiser steps with no augmentation; 16 frames instead of 32; a test subset that excludes 15 identities and includes the 300 YouTube real videos.
- What our implementation contributes: an identity-disjoint split with a sealed test touched once; three seeds and group-bootstrap intervals; a re-run published baseline under one recipe, which shows that the same Xception scores 0.8527 here against 73.7 percent in R-021 Table 2, so quoted baselines are recipe-dependent; the staircase from I-0 (0.6238) to I-1 (0.8218) on the family.
- Most important next improvement: parameter-efficient adaptation of a larger CLIP encoder (the Effort or Forensics Adapter route, which train 0.19M and 5.7M parameters and therefore fit our card), evaluated on the same sealed split; and, for comparability, an additional evaluation on the official Celeb-DF v2 test list.

### 4.2 image_detector (T-I)

- Closest published benchmarks: UnivFD linear probe on the same encoder (total mAP 93.38, R-011), CNNDetection (83.58 mAP as listed by UnivFD), Effort (99.41 mAP).
- Our result: ProGAN 1.0000, CNN units 0.9316 [0.8933, 0.9694], diffusion subset 0.9354 [0.8967, 0.9665] (E-014).
- Gap where comparable: none computed (different generator coverage and training size). Metric-matched since 2026-10-27: mean AP over the 8 diffusion sets is 0.9387 against 95.00 mAP for the same group in R-011 (category B, section 3.2); mean AP over our 6 CNN-synthesis folders is 0.9714 (the paper's GAN group is 11 sets, 99.31).
- Likely reasons for differences: 8,000 against 720,000 training images; pooled AUC over units against a mean of per-set AP.
- Contribution: the UnivFD recipe reproduced at about 1 percent of the training data, with group intervals that make the dependence on few generator units visible.
- Most important next improvement: modern generators (Community Forensics: E-028 measured the frozen head as INADEQUATE on them, E-029 raised the macro AUC to 0.884 by retraining on 125 recent generators but its threshold flags about half of the real images, so it stays out of the product); reporting AP per generator beside AUC so that the published tables become partially or directly comparable.

### 4.3 speech (T-A)

- Closest published benchmarks: Pascu et al. (frozen encoder plus logistic regression, In-the-Wild EER 7.2 with a 2B encoder, 38.6 with WavLM base plus, R-083), Muller et al. (33.9 best, R-033), AASIST (1.13 mean EER in-corpus, R-012).
- Our result: In-the-Wild sealed AUC 0.9148 [0.8896, 0.9383], EER 0.1578 [0.1269, 0.1883]; ASVspoof 2019 LA eval AUC 0.9835 [0.9798, 0.9872], EER 0.0694 [0.0595, 0.0782] (E-014; EER from E-030, post hoc).
- Gap where comparable: against our own implementation of the published frozen-encoder recipe family (I-0), +0.1633 [+0.1177, +0.2266] on In-the-Wild (T2). Against AASIST on the same official ASVspoof 2019 LA eval with matched training data and EER (E-030, post hoc): 6.94 percent against 1.13 percent, +5.81 points (worse). Against Pascu et al. on the same ASVspoof 2019 LA test split (R-083, trained on LA train and dev): XLS-R 2B 0.5 percent (ours +6.44 points, worse) and RawNet2 5.9 percent (ours +1.04 points, worse in point estimate, small). In-the-Wild EER 0.1578 [0.1269, 0.1883] is metric-matched with R-033 and R-083 but on 42 of 54 speakers, so no gap is computed.
- Exploratory agreement: on the development slice our frozen WavLM base plus head has EER 0.3930 and wav2vec 2.0 base 0.5020 (E-003), the same ordering as Pascu et al. Table 3 (38.6 and 45.9 on the full set); not differenced.
- Likely reasons: our gains came from preprocessing (silence trim, peak normalisation), nuisance augmentation and averaging three encoders; R-083 indicates that encoder scale is a larger lever, which we did not use.
- Contribution: an iteration staircase on a sealed out-of-corpus test (0.7516, 0.8125, 0.8672, 0.9148) with speaker-group intervals; a calibration finding (temperature fitted in-corpus makes out-of-corpus scores worse, E-004).
- Most important next improvement: (EER now computed, E-030; min t-DCF not computed) a larger frozen encoder (XLS-R 300M or 1B) with the same head.

### 4.4 lip_sync

- Closest published benchmarks: SyncNet's own offset accuracy (above 99 percent per clip, human-judged, R-084); Wav2Lip's SyncNet scores (R-085); AVAD as a sync-based detector (R-026).
- Our result: accurate offset measurement of known shifts (97.8 percent [94.4, 99.1], E-024); false alarms 15.0 percent under the plus or minus 2 frame rule and 1.3 percent [0.4, 3.8] under the calibrated band (E-024, E-025); 0 of 160 Wav2Lip clips flagged under the band (E-025).
- Gap: no comparable published metric.
- Contribution: a measured false-alarm rate for SyncNet offsets on unmodified real speech, which the papers read do not report, and the demonstration that the offset cue does not separate Wav2Lip fakes once false alarms are controlled.
- Most important next improvement: if lip sync is to become a detector, a learned model over the full time-delay distribution in the manner of AVAD (R-026), trained on real videos only and evaluated with a false-alarm control; otherwise keep it as weak evidence.

### 4.5 generated_video

- Closest published benchmarks: DF26 Table 5 (AUROC 44.0 to 69.7 for FF++-trained detectors, R-081); DeepAction (97.0 percent accuracy, R-082); ReStraV (AUROC 98.63 on VidProM, R-078).
- Our result: DeepAction LOGO AUC 0.8281 [0.7908, 0.8662] (development, E-020), DF26 0.4957 [0.4755, 0.5168] (all) and 0.4880 [0.4534, 0.5204] (closed) (E-020); 77 percent of real clips from other sources above the threshold (E-026).
- Gap where comparable: on the DF26 zero-shot protocol, -20.1 AUROC points against the best published detector (GenD-PE), +5.6 against Effort (point estimates, published values without intervals). Both our value and several published values are near chance.
- Likely reasons: the head learned DeepAction's real-source appearance (E-026); DeepAction's 100 real clips come from one stock library; DF26 is talking heads, DeepAction is action footage; 2 s at 224 px discards detail.
- Contribution: the distinction between a development AUC that is source-confounded (0.8281) and a held-out AUC at chance (0.4880), made explicit by a preregistered source-confound test; a canonical re-encode that removes container shortcuts before any training.
- E-027 (2026-10-27): in-domain training on DF26's open generators with a family-held-out protocol raised the closed-subset AUC to 0.7548 [0.7189, 0.7885] (from 0.4880 for C2); temporal features added nothing (PT14 minus P14 -0.0404 [-0.0650, -0.0165]); the deployable head still flags 0.8023 [0.7625, 0.8368] of FF++ and LAV-DF real clips at its balanced threshold, so the source confound persists.
- Most important next improvement: real training clips from many sources (including FF++-like and LAV-DF-like footage) so that the real class is not one or two libraries, with the same source-confound control as the acceptance test; a head is only a candidate decider when that control passes.

### 4.6 Audio-visual mode and provenance

- No fused verdict is claimed. The LAV-DF fused AUC (0.9945) is partially comparable with BA-TFD (0.990) but is explained by a production cue; FakeAVCeleb numbers are inflated by a codec shortcut in the data (E-021), which none of the published FakeAVCeleb fusion papers read controls for.
- C2PA has no accuracy benchmark; our wording follows the specification's own scope statement (R-086).

## 5. Gap analysis

1. Metric mismatch is the largest obstacle to comparison. Our headline metric is AUC everywhere; audio papers report EER (R-012, R-033, R-083), image papers report AP and accuracy (R-011, R-035, R-038, R-047), and DeepAction reports accuracy (R-082). Only DF26 and the face-forgery papers use AUC. Adding EER for audio and AP for images would turn several partially comparable rows into directly comparable ones without new training. (Update 2026-10-27: EER for audio exists (E-030; it is also stored in the dfd-eval-report/1 files of 2026-10-25) and made the ASVspoof rows category A; AP for images was computed from the stored scores for the diffusion and CNN-synthesis units, which moved those rows closer but not to A because the release and the real pairing differ.)
2. Test-subset mismatch on the main face benchmark. Our Celeb-DF v2 test deliberately excludes 15 development identities and does not use the official list (D-035). This protects the sealed test but means no published Celeb-DF v2 number is directly comparable. A one-time evaluation of the frozen system on the official 518-video list would fix this (needs a decision, since some of those videos are in our development slice). Measured on 2026-10-27: 119 of the 518 official-list videos (28 real, 91 fake) are in our development slice and 399 are in test_x, so the official list cannot be evaluated as a whole without using development identities; the 399-video part is reported in section 3.1 (category B).
3. Aggregation mismatch. DeepfakeBench and part of Forensics Adapter report frame-level AUC; we report clip-level AUC from 16 frames. Our E-004 battery logged both for dev_x (frame 0.656, clip mean 0.685 for I-0), but not for the sealed sets.
4. Scale gap. The strongest published numbers in every task come from larger encoders (CLIP ViT-L/14 adapted, XLS-R 2B, ViT on thousands of generators). Our 12 GB card limits full fine-tuning to base-size encoders; parameter-efficient methods are the published way around this.
5. Where we contribute evidence the literature lacks: false-alarm rates of SyncNet offsets on real speech (E-024, E-025); a source-confound test for a generated-video head (E-026); codec and metadata shortcut audits on FakeAVCeleb and DeepAction (E-018, E-021, E-023); seeds and group-bootstrap intervals for every number.

## 6. Viva-ready findings and limitations

- "How does your video detector compare with the literature?" Under the same protocol as a re-run Xception, the final system is non-inferior on the sealed Celeb-DF family (+0.0176 [+0.0025, +0.0327]; not robust at the Bonferroni level). Against published detectors on Celeb-DF v2 and DFD the comparison is partial because our test subset differs; the published CLIP-based methods report higher values (0.956 and 0.965 for Effort) and we state that as a difference in conditions, not as a measured gap.
- "Is your In-the-Wild result better than Muller et al.'s 33.9 percent EER?" Not established as a computed gap: our EER is 15.8 percent [12.7, 18.8] (E-030, post hoc) on 42 of 54 speakers, theirs 33.9 percent on the full set. The metric now matches but the speaker set does not, so we say our number is lower on our subset and do not claim better.
- "Why did your generated-video head score 0.83 and then 0.49?" 0.8281 is leave-one-generator-out AUC on DeepAction, a development corpus whose 100 real clips come from one stock library; 0.4880 [0.4534, 0.5204] is the held-out AUC on DF26's closed generators. E-026 then showed that 77 percent of real clips from FF++ and LAV-DF score above the head's threshold, so the DeepAction number reflects source and content, not generation. The module is experimental and always inconclusive.
- "Why is lip sync not a detector?" Because unmodified real speech already spreads from about -6 to +2 frames (E-025), and Wav2Lip output sits inside that spread (0 of 160 flagged), which agrees with Wav2Lip being trained against a SyncNet expert (R-085, Table 1).
- "Are FakeAVCeleb numbers trustworthy?" They are ranking quality on a dataset with a codec shortcut: codec metadata alone separates real from fake video at AUC 0.9985 (E-021), and our visual AUC falls from 0.9732 to 0.8772 after a common re-encode.
- Limitations of this benchmark file: most external rows are partially comparable or context only; published values have no intervals; several papers are PARTIAL in RESEARCH.md (only the sections listed were read); table values that were scrambled in text extraction were either checked against printed averages (DeepfakeBench Table 3, Community Forensics) or not quoted.

## 10. Master tables by modality (added 2026-10-27, agent E)

Reading rules.

- Rows marked "Ours" are our measurements; rows marked "Published" are numbers printed in the cited paper (R-ID and table or section in the Source column) or, where the Source says so, quoted by a second paper. The two kinds are never combined in one cell.
- "Not reported" = the source does not give the metric in the parts read; "N/A" = not computed for our rows (no stored result gives it) or not applicable. Nothing is estimated.
- Interval = 95 percent bootstrap interval of the stored result (group bootstrap over identities, speakers, units or generators as the experiment states). Published values carry no interval unless written.
- Threshold-dependent columns of our rows (precision, recall/TPR, FPR, accuracy) are at the frozen threshold of that system, not at 0.5. For the E-014 sealed blocks this is the per-seed threshold chosen on the validation split (maximum balanced accuracy), averaged over 3 seeds. Precision, recall/TPR and EER of E-014 rows are the stored values in data/_final/eval/<tag>.json. Values marked † (FPR and accuracy) were computed on 2026-10-27 from the stored sealed score file data/_final/scores/<tag>.csv at those thresholds by the script in section 12; the same script reproduces the stored precision, recall and balanced accuracy of all 22 tag and block combinations it was run on (checked against the JSON, section 12), which validates the threshold convention. Accuracy depends on class balance (for example 9.7 percent real in the Celeb-DF family, 89.7 percent spoofed in ASVspoof 2019 LA) and must be read with FPR. Values marked ‡ are arithmetic on stored results: exact when they use a stored confusion matrix (E-027) or the stored predictions file with labels from the manifest (E-015, script in section 12), and derived from the stored sensitivity, specificity and class counts when no confusion matrix is stored (E-020 on DF26; the stored rates recover integer counts, TP 766 and TN 178).
- Aggregation level is stated in the protocol column: face video = video score is the mean of the probabilities of 16 face crops per video; speech = per utterance (first 64,600 samples scored); generated image = per whole image unless "face crop" is stated; generated video = per clip (8 or 14 canonical frames, first 2 s); FakeAVCeleb = per clip.
- Comparability letters are defined in section 1 (A direct, B same dataset different protocol, C cross-dataset or out-of-distribution, D contextual).

### 10.7 What the experimental detectors do and do not show (Phase 6 statement)

- E-028, frozen generated-image detector (T-I, as shipped): on 11,550 images of 21 modern generators it has macro AUC 0.7039 [0.6855, 0.7229], and it misses 73.95 percent of the generated images at its frozen threshold (pooled false-negative rate 0.7395 [0.7300, 0.7500]; pooled false-positive rate 0.0418). Verdict by the preregistered line: INADEQUATE. Nothing here is a statement about face-swap detection: T-I is a whole-image generated-image detector trained on ProGAN; the face-swap detector is a different model with a different task.
- E-029, same recipe retrained on 125 recent generators: macro AUC seed mean 0.8839 (frozen candidate seed 0: 0.8863 [0.8756, 0.8965]), a ranking gain of +0.1800 [+0.1643, +0.1959] over the frozen head on the same images. At its own validation threshold the frozen candidate flags 52.13 percent [50.91, 53.39] of the real images (LAION photographs 58.85 percent, COCO 3.28 percent). It is not eligible for the product (D-086) and is not deployment-ready; no threshold that works across real sources was found or tested, because choosing one on the evaluation images would be tuning on the test set.
- E-027, generated-video head trained on DF26's open generators: closed-generator AUC 0.7548 [0.7189, 0.7885] against unseen generators (reals reused from development), but 353 of 440 = 0.8023 [0.7625, 0.8368] unmodified real videos from FF++ and LAV-DF are flagged at its balanced threshold, so a high DF26 AUC is not evidence about real-world videos. It is not installed (D-085).
- A generated-image detector (E-028, E-029) and a face-swap detector (T-V) answer different questions; the two are compared only inside E-028's face analysis, where the face-swap path is the one the product currently applies to still images with a face, and neither is usable there.

## 13. Conclusions (added 2026-10-27, agent E)

Short answers to the nine questions of the benchmark audit. Numbers are the stored ones named in sections 9 to 12. Differences are given only for category A rows.

### 13.1 Which datasets have been evaluated?

Sealed or held-out tests with stored scores: FF++ c23 test; Celeb-DF v2 test_x; Celeb-DF++ fakes with Celeb-DF v2 reals; DFD c23 (all); ForenSynths ProGAN test, the CNN-synthesis test_x (6 generator folders) and the UnivFD diffusion subset; ASVspoof 2019 LA official evaluation set; In-the-Wild (42 sealed speakers); LAV-DF (balanced 2,000-video test subset); FakeAVCeleb v1.2 (sealed identity half, once); DF26 (all 2,691 clips, once in E-020; the closed subset a second time in E-027); an 11,550-image subset of Community Forensics-Eval (E-028, E-029). Never used: DFDC (verified, section 9.3), WildDeepfake, FF++ c40, AV-Deepfake1M.

### 13.2 Which results can be reused without rerunning inference?

All E-014 sealed blocks (score files data/_final/scores/*.csv with hashes; any threshold-free metric, ROC points, FPR at a chosen TPR, AP per unit and paired bootstrap differences can be recomputed; this was done for FPR, accuracy, AP and the official-list part in section 12), the FakeAVCeleb predictions (data/_results/e015/predictions_test_x.csv), the DF26 closed scores (data/_results/modern/e027/closed_scores.csv, rounded to 6 decimals) and features, the Community Forensics per-image caches (data/_results/modern/e028/cache, e029/cache_eval15), and the lip-sync per-clip records. What cannot be reused: frame-level scores of the sealed video sets (the reports have n_frame_rows 0), which a frame-level AUC for the DeepfakeBench comparison would need; 32-frame video scores; any metric on data that was never scored.

### 13.3 Where is additional evaluation genuinely necessary?

To turn category B rows into A, in order of value for the face task:

1. Celeb-DF v2 official test list (518 videos) with 32 frames per video, video level and frame level. 399 of the 518 are sealed test_x videos scored with 16 frames, 119 are development videos; the published CLIP-based detectors (Effort 0.956, Forensics Adapter 0.957) and SBI (0.9318) are all on this list.
2. DFDC: the dataset of the DeepfakeBench columns and of most published tables; never obtained (access route and rules unverified, STATUS.md blocker 4). Needs a decision before any use.
3. Full In-the-Wild (all speakers): needed for the Muller and Pascu rows; the 12 development speakers would enter the number, so it would be an optimistic bound, or the published comparison stays B.
4. Community Forensics Eval, Comprehensive version, per-generator AP and accuracy: needed to compare with mAP 0.987 and accuracy 0.892; only 11,550 images of the set were scored.
5. Fresh real clips from many sources for the generated-video heads (acceptance test of E-026, E-027): not a literature comparison, but without it no generated-video head can pass its own acceptance line.

Not necessary for the claims this project makes: further ASVspoof 2019 LA evaluation (already the full official set), further FF++ in-dataset evaluation, ProGAN test.

In particular: Celeb-DF v2 and DFD against Effort, SBI, Forensics Adapter and LipForensics (other subsets and frame counts, other training compression, no intervals); frame-level DeepfakeBench AUC against our video-level AUC; In-the-Wild EER on 42 of 54 speakers against the full set of Muller et al. and Pascu et al.; UnivFD and Effort mAP and accuracy against our pooled AUC and our smaller generator coverage; Community Forensics mAP 0.987 against our 11,550-image subset (E-028, E-029); DeepAction accuracy against our leave-one-generator-out AUC; BA-TFD on the full LAV-DF test against our 2,000-video subset; every FakeAVCeleb fusion paper (in-dataset, codec shortcut) against our zero-shot per-modality scores; all lip-sync and C2PA rows (no published counterpart). A generated-image detector (E-028, E-029) is never ranked against a face-swap detector: the task differs.

### 13.7 Strongest defensible claims for the professor

1. The evaluation protocol is sound and auditable: split once by source identity, sealed test read once per system, a ledger and a guard in the evaluation script, three seeds, group-bootstrap intervals, a re-run published baseline under the same recipe. 28 of 28 spot-checks of headline numbers match their stored files.
2. Face video: on a sealed cross-dataset family (Celeb-DF v2 plus Celeb-DF++, 7,650 videos, 344 identity groups) the fine-tuned SigLIP base head reaches AUC 0.8218 [0.8020, 0.8393], clearly above the frozen working version (+0.1980 [+0.1688, +0.2242]) and not distinguished from a re-run Xception at the Bonferroni level (+0.0176 [+0.0025, +0.0327]; non-inferior with delta 0.0100). In-dataset both are at 0.99.
3. Speech: the staircase I-0 to I-4 raised the sealed out-of-corpus AUC from 0.7516 to 0.9148 (+0.1633 [+0.1177, +0.2266]); EER on that set is 15.78 percent [12.69, 18.83]. In-corpus the system is worse than published specialised or larger-encoder systems (EER 6.94 against 1.13, 0.5 and 5.9 percent), a trade that is measured and stated.
4. Generated images: the UnivFD-style recipe reproduced at 1 percent of the training data gives mean AP 0.9387 on the eight diffusion sets (UnivFD group value 95.00, category B), and the module is correctly labelled inadequate on modern generators (E-028: macro AUC 0.7039, 74 percent missed). Retraining on many recent generators improved the ranking (+0.1800 [+0.1643, +0.1959]) but the threshold flags 52 percent of real images, so it is not shipped.
5. Evidence the literature read does not provide: a codec shortcut in FakeAVCeleb (metadata alone AUC 0.9985; visual AUC 0.9732 falls to 0.8772 after a common re-encode); a source-confound test showing that generated-video heads flag 77 to 80 percent of unmodified real FF++ and LAV-DF clips; a false-alarm rate of SyncNet offsets on real speech (3 of 230 under the calibrated band) and the result that a band calibrated on real clips does not flag Wav2Lip (0 of 160).
6. Generated video is handled honestly: zero-shot AUC 0.4957 on DF26 (the published FF++-trained detectors are between 44.0 and 69.7), a trained head that reaches 0.7548 on unseen closed generators but is source-confounded and therefore not installed.

### 13.8 Most important limitations

- No category A row exists for the face-video task against any published detector; the Celeb-DF v2 official list is only 77 percent available as sealed data, DFDC was never used, frames per video are 16 against 32, and published values have no intervals.
- Thresholds do not transfer: at the frozen threshold the final video system has TPR 0.59 and FPR 0.11 on the Celeb-DF family, TPR 0.48 on FakeAVCeleb, and the speech system flags 17 percent of real In-the-Wild clips (the I-0 head 84 percent); AUC alone overstates deployability.
- Accuracy is not informative on these sets (9.7 percent real in the Celeb-DF family, 89.7 percent spoofed in ASVspoof 2019 LA).
- Base-size encoders only (12 GB card); the published leaders use CLIP ViT-L/14 adaptation, XLS-R 2B or thousands of generators.
- Datasets carry artefacts (codec in FakeAVCeleb, container and colour tags in DeepAction, PNG against JPEG in Community Forensics-Eval, production cue in LAV-DF) that inflate AUC; controls reduce but do not remove them.
- Several results are exploratory or post hoc by label (LAV-DF, E-030, the official-list part, the AP values), the E-027 closed look is the second look at that subset with reused reals, and E-028 and E-029 use an 11,550-image subset of which 82.5 percent of the reals are LAION photographs.
- The generated-video and generated-image experimental heads are not deployment-ready; E-029 is not eligible for the product (D-086) and E-027 is not installed (D-085).

### 13.9 Smallest additional experiment that would materially strengthen the evaluation

Score the frozen face system (I-1 video head, no retraining, no threshold change) on the 518 videos of the official Celeb-DF v2 test list (data/celebdf_v2/List_of_testing_videos.txt: 178 real, 340 fake; 399 of them are sealed test_x videos, 119 are development videos), with 32 evenly spaced face-crop frames per video, 3 seeds, and report video-level AUC (mean of frame probabilities) and frame-level AUC, each with an identity-group bootstrap interval, next to the re-run Xception (BASE) scored the same way. Write the hypothesis and the success line first (the line is for the main agent to set; no value is proposed here). Because 399 videos are sealed, a second look at them with a changed frame count needs a decision and a `--repeat` entry with a reason in docs/STATUS.md under the freeze protocol (docs/EXPERIMENTS.md E-014: "A second final evaluation of a tag needs --repeat with a reason"); the 119 development videos carry no such restriction. It costs about 518 videos times 3 seeds of inference. Effect: the Celeb-DF v2 rows against Effort (0.956), Forensics Adapter (0.957), SBI (0.9318), LipForensics (0.824) and DeepfakeBench (0.7365, frame level) move from category B to A (same list, same metric, same aggregation level, same FF++ c23 training family for Effort and Forensics Adapter), and the frame-level AUC answers the DeepfakeBench column. If the repeat is not authorised, the post hoc 399-video value of section 3.1 (0.8478 [0.7998, 0.8884]) is the closest available number and stays category B.
