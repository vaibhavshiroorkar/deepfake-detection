# C1: audio-visual, lip-sync and audio anti-spoofing literature

Stream C1 (literature). Written 2026-10-24. This file is an input for the integrator; it is not a living document and it does not edit RESEARCH.md or DECISIONS.md. IDs C1-01 to C1-14 are temporary and must be renumbered when merged.

## Method and protocol notes

- Every paper below was downloaded as a PDF (arXiv), converted with `pdftotext -layout`, and read from that text. Each arXiv identifier was checked against the title page of the downloaded file (all matched the intended title; no wrong-paper downloads this time). PDFs and text are kept outside the repository, in the C1 scratch folder.
- C1-01 (Zhou and Lim) is the same paper as R-020 in RESEARCH.md (status there TO READ). It was read in full here, so R-020 can be upgraded when merged.
- pdftotext garbles many result tables (rows and columns separate). Only numbers whose row and column could be recovered with confidence are recorded. Where a table was ambiguous this is stated in the Limitations cell.
- Venue rule: where the file itself does not print a venue it says "not printed in the file, to verify". Where I saw a venue only in a search result I name it as a search result, not as verified.
- No number here is a result of this project. All are published numbers, each with its protocol.
- Metric words: ACC accuracy, AUC area under ROC, AP average precision, EER equal error rate, min t-DCF minimum tandem detection cost, AR average recall.
- "In-dataset" means train and test are drawn from the same dataset family. "Cross-dataset" means train on one dataset and test on another. "Cross-category" or "cross-generator" means a manipulation type or attack algorithm is held out of training.

## (b) Findings that bear on open decisions

Each finding names the protocol its number was measured under. None of this chooses anything.

### O-001 (problem statement and output)

1. Separate labels per modality are used by the audio-visual detectors that report them: video label, audio label and whole-sequence label (C1-01), or the four combinations real/fake audio and video (C1-03, C1-04, C1-05). The datasets that carry such labels are FakeAVCeleb (R-014), LAV-DF (C1-05), AV-Deepfake1M (C1-06) and the vocoder-resynthesised FF++/DFDC set of C1-01 that its authors built; KoDF has real audio only (C1-04, C1-07, C1-08), so it cannot test an audio branch.
2. A visual-only branch cannot see audio-only fakes. In-dataset (LAV-DF identity-independent test split), BA-TFD localisation AP@0.5 is 58.55 for the visual-only variant against 76.90 multimodal on the full test set, and 83.55 against 85.20 once audio-only fakes are removed (C1-05).
3. Per-category results show where a single fused score hides weakness. In-dataset, FakeAVCeleb balanced 5-fold identity-independent (C1-03): the fused baseline gets 70.0 percent accuracy on real audio with fake video and 86.8 on real/real, but 99.6 to 99.8 on the fake-audio categories; the regularised version lifts the real-audio-fake-video category to 87.4. Cross-category (leave one FakeAVCeleb category out, AUC, C1-07): a sync-only unsupervised detector reaches 71.6 to 80.5 on fake audio with real video and 92.8 to 94.5 on fake-visual categories.
4. Temporal localisation is feasible in-dataset but collapses on harder data: BA-TFD AP@0.5 79.15 (LAV-DF, in-dataset) against 37.37 (AV-Deepfake1M, in-dataset), BA-TFD+ 96.30 against 44.42, UMMAFormer 98.83 against 51.64 (C1-06). Option (c) in O-001 (localisation) would need a dataset with segment labels; only LAV-DF and AV-Deepfake1M here have them.

### O-002 (how image, video and audio inputs are handled; what claims each supports)

1. Still images: LipForensics cannot be applied to isolated images (C1-02, authors' limitation); AVFF needs both audio and video (C1-04, authors' limitation). So none of the lip-sync or audio-visual detectors read covers a still image or a video without audio.
2. Audio: the best in-corpus EER among twelve re-implemented models is about 1.2 percent (ASVspoof 2019 evaluation) and the best out-of-corpus EER is about 33.9 percent on real-world clips, achieved by different models (cross-database, train ASVspoof 2019 LA, test In-the-Wild, EER, mean of 3 seeds; many other models near chance, C1-14). On a modern, speaker-disjoint, compressed benchmark, RawNet2 and AASIST baselines reach 36.04 and 29.12 percent pooled EER (train and test on ASVspoof 5, attacks unseen; C1-12). Any audio claim has to name the corpus and the compression.
3. Waveform versus spectrogram: in ASVspoof 2019 evaluation (cross-attack, same corpus) raw-waveform models reached the best EER (about 1.2 percent), constant-Q or log spectrograms beat mel spectrograms by 37 percent EER on average, and full-length input beat 4 s crops (C1-14). In ASVspoof 2021 (train 2019, test 2021, codec shift) the pooled wav2vec 2.0 front end halved EER relative to the sinc-layer front end under identical heads (6.15 against 11.47, best seed; C1-10). A classical LFCC-GMM was more robust across generators than RawNet2 trained on one vocoder (cross-generator, single-speaker corpus, C1-13) and better pooled than RawNet2 on ASVspoof 2019 (C1-09).
4. Video: a frozen lipreading-pretrained temporal model with a small head reached cross-manipulation mean AUC 97.1 and cross-dataset mean AUC 87.7 (train FF++, video-level; C1-02) and it is the lowest on DFDC (73.5) and Celeb-DF v2 (82.4), so face-swap datasets with unusual filming conditions remain hard.

### O-005 (metrics, threshold policy, calibration)

1. Audio benchmarks use EER and min t-DCF; min t-DCF needs a speaker-verification system, and the 2021 deepfake task dropped it and used EER (C1-11). ASVspoof 5 reports EER for detectors and a-DCF for joint systems, and notes that the minimum versions use an oracle threshold that is unavailable in practice, leaving threshold selection for future work (C1-12). This matches the project's rule that thresholds come from validation data only.
2. Progress-set tuning can mislead. ASVspoof 2021 deepfake task: best EER 0.10 percent on the progress subset, 15.6 percent on the evaluation partition (C1-11).
3. Best-seed versus mean differs. wav2vec 2.0 front end with aggregation and augmentation: 0.82 percent EER best seed, 1.00 mean of three, on ASVspoof 2021 LA with training on 2019 LA (C1-10). On In-the-Wild, seed standard deviation reaches about 15 EER points (C1-14).
4. Aggregation rule: frame or segment scores to a video score by max, mean or mean of top five changed the AV-Deepfake1M AUC of the same Xception model between 61.44 and 68.81 (in-dataset, full test; C1-06). LipForensics averaged clip predictions over the whole video so that frame-based and clip-based models used the same number of frames (C1-02).
5. Label definition for a video-only baseline matters: AVFF counted a video as fake only if the visual track was fake when scoring visual-only detectors (C1-04); C1-07 does not report modality-incompatible categories for such detectors. A comparison across detectors with different modality scope needs this stated.
6. Class balance: Zhou and Lim balanced the four label combinations in test (C1-01); MRDF balanced them 1:1:1:1 (C1-03), so only a quarter of the test set is real; AV-Deepfake1M test is 85,820 real and 257,420 fake (C1-06); LAV-DF is about 27 percent real (C1-05).

### O-007 (candidate backbones per modality)

1. Audio: AASIST-L (R-012) has no measurement here, but its lineage does: RawNet2 (C1-09) did worse pooled than a hand-crafted LFCC-GMM baseline on ASVspoof 2019 evaluation (min t-DCF 0.1175 to 0.1301 against 0.0904), was complementary under fusion (0.0330 to 0.0443), and was better on the worst attack (A17). A wav2vec 2.0 XLS-R front end gave the lowest EERs reported in its paper (6.15 down to 0.82 percent on 2021 LA, cross-condition) but is large and was pre-trained on extra external data (C1-10). A small classical baseline is worth including (C1-13).
2. Video: a frozen pretrained network with a small trainable head is competitive on cross-manipulation tests and robust to compression (C1-02, mean AUC under corruption 92.5 against 67.5 to 84.1 for five conventional detectors). 24.8M trainable parameters for the full ResNet-18 plus temporal net, with the extractor frozen (C1-02 Table 6).
3. Detectors that look strong on one audio-visual set can fall on another: EfficientViT AUC 96.50 on LAV-DF (in-dataset) against 65.51 on AV-Deepfake1M (in-dataset), Xception 83.58 against 68.68 (C1-06). A candidate comparison on a single dataset can therefore overstate differences.

### O-009 (fusion strategy)

1. Joint training with late fusion of pooled features was not better than independently trained streams combined by an OR rule (in-dataset FF++ whole-sequence accuracy 94.25 against 95.83; DFDC 89.15 against 89.36; C1-01). Mid-level fusion with a dedicated label for the combined stream was better in-dataset (96.2 to 97.6 on FF++, about 90.3 to 91.0 on DFDC) and under a held-out category (whole-sequence accuracy 85.2 to 88.0 against 73.4 to 87.0 for independent streams on four held-out pairs; C1-01).
2. On FakeAVCeleb, same data and backbone: a soft-voting ensemble of unimodal audio (AUC 0.813) and visual (0.809) detectors gave 0.844, feature concatenation 0.890, and a subspace fusion with orthogonality constraints 0.973 (same-dataset test, C1-08). With explicit per-modality regularisation, 5-fold identity-independent ACC / AUC 94.05 / 92.43 against 89.05 / 88.30 for plain fused training (C1-03). These contrast with the weak ensembles in the dataset paper itself (R-014, ensembles near 50 AUC); the protocols differ, so do not read them as a contradiction.
3. A pretrained audio-visual correspondence model (no fake data in pre-training) gave 99.1 AUC in-dataset and 95.5 AUC on a KoDF subset trained on FakeAVCeleb (C1-04), but it supports only clips with both modalities.
4. Missing modality: no paper read handles an absent track inside the model. The evidence on what is lost: a visual-only variant of a joint model loses 18.4 AP points on a set with audio-only fakes (C1-05); C1-08 reports only visual and multimodal scores on KoDF because it has no fake audio. Handling a still image or silent video would therefore need either per-modality branches whose scores are combined only when both are present (as in the independent-stream design of C1-01), or an explicit decision to give no audio claim for such input.
5. A synchronisation-only cue misses fake audio that stays in sync: AUC 71.6 to 80.5 for real video with fake audio in an unsupervised detector (C1-07), against 92.8 to 94.5 on categories with fake video.
