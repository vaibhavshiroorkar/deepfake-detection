# Stream C3: rPPG on current generators, rPPG extraction methods, surveys

Written 2026-10-24 by Stream C3. Not committed. Temp IDs C3-01 to C3-18 are for the integrator to map to R-numbers.

How the papers were read: each PDF was fetched with curl into `scratchpad\streamC3\pdf`, converted with `pdftotext` (layout and raw modes) into `streamC3\txt` and `streamC3\raw`, and the matrix was filled from that text. Every arXiv ID was checked against the downloaded title page (all matched the intended title). Paywalled or bot-blocked sources were not bypassed. Search-engine snippets were used only to find files, never as evidence. Numbers keep the protocol named in the same cell; in-dataset and cross-dataset values are never merged. Where a table in the extracted text lost its column order, the row says so.

Status vocabulary: READ, PARTIAL (sections listed), NR, TO READ.

## (b) Findings bearing on D-006 and O-010

All findings carry their protocol. "In-dataset" means train and test from the same dataset or construction; "cross-generator" means the generator differs; "cross-dataset" means the dataset differs.

### B1. What each pulse-extraction method needs (to size O-010)

| Method (source) | Window and input | Frame rate | Video quality it was shown on |
|---|---|---|---|
| POS (C3-08) | Sliding window of 32 frames (1.6 s at 20 fps), overlap-add, no band-pass in the bare algorithm; heart-rate estimation then needs a longer spectrum | 20 fps in the authors' benchmark | Uncompressed bitmap 768x576; the authors call MPEG4-compressed MAHNOB-HCI unsuitable |
| POS and CHROM in a toolbox (C3-09) | Whole-video heart rate; Butterworth 0.75 to 2.5 Hz then FFT or peak picking | 30 fps | MAE about 4 bpm on clean UBFC-rPPG and PURE, about 12 to 14 bpm on compressed phone video (MMPD, 320x240) |
| PhysFormer (C3-06) | Clips of 160 frames at 128x128 (about 5.3 s at 30 fps); tested on 10 s clips | 30 fps (60 and 61 fps sets downsampled) | VIPL-HR, MAHNOB-HCI, MMSE-HR, OBF; high-compression MAHNOB was hard enough to need fine-tuning |
| RhythmFormer (C3-07) | 160 frames best in the authors' ablation; 128x128 crop from the first frame | 30 fps checkpoint; 20 fps COHFACE | Works on heavily compressed COHFACE only with the fusion stem |
| DeepRhythm (C3-05) | First 300 frames, 25 face blocks, motion magnification | Not stated | FF++ (level not stated) and DFDC-preview |
| rPPG-derived 1D ResNet over RhythmFormer (C3-15, R-009) | Windows of 160 frames, stride 60 for real videos, one window per fake | Native fps (30 real, 24 to 25 fake) | Celeb-DF++ talking-face videos |
| Map-based classifier (C3-17) | 6 regions, first 300 frames, clips mostly over 5 s | Not stated | FF++, DFDC, Celeb-DF v2 |
| Deepfake classifier on PhysFormer tokens (C3-16) | 160-frame clips | 20 and 30 fps | 640x480 rPPG datasets |

Arithmetic from the above (mine, not from a paper): a 160-frame window is about 5.3 s at 30 fps and 6.7 s at 24 fps; a spectrum from a window of T seconds has a raw frequency step of 1/T Hz, so 5.3 s gives roughly 11 bpm bins before interpolation. Real videos in Celeb-DF++ average about 13 s and the fakes about 10 s (C3-04), so a 160-frame window fits in each; DFDC clips are 10 s (R-003 and the DFDC text re-checked for this note), so one non-overlapping 160-frame window per clip, or about three with a stride of 60 frames.

### B2. Video compression and rPPG (the "tiny colour changes" concern)

- In-dataset, lab data, ICA, x264 (C3-10): pulse SNR falls from -7.80 dB (raw) to -11.3 dB at CRF 18 and -12.3 dB at CRF 24, and pulse-rate error rises from 1.78 bpm to 3.35 and 4.38 bpm (stationary task). With random head motion the error rises from 1.18 bpm (raw) to 8.66 bpm at CRF 18. The paper notes CRF 18 to 28 is the usual visual range.
- Same-compression train and test, deep model (C3-12): heart-rate error changes little up to CRF 15, grows clearly beyond CRF 20 (about 450 kb/s for 640x480 at 30 fps); cross-dataset error is much higher even at small CRF.
- Cross-codec, deep models (C3-11): all methods lose accuracy as bit rate drops from the original about 20,000 kb/s to 1000, 500, 250 kb/s (MPEG4, x264, x265); numeric values in figures only.
- Dataset bit rates (C3-12): uncompressed rPPG sets are 215 to 295 Mb/s; MAHNOB-HCI at 4.2 Mb/s and COHFACE at 0.25 Mb/s are the "highly compressed" benchmarks in which extractors are known to struggle (C3-06, C3-07).
- What this means for FF++ / Celeb-DF / DFDC: none of the papers read states the bit rates of those three datasets. What is printed: FF++ source videos were YouTube downloads of 480p or higher, released at raw, H.264 "constant rate quantization parameter equal to 23" (c23) and 40 (c40) (R-001 text re-checked); Celeb-DF real videos are MPEG4 YouTube material with generated videos in MPEG4 (R-002, C3-04); DFDC source video was mostly 1080p, 10 s clips, with faces swapped at 256x256 and pasted back, and part of the test videos perturbed (frame-rate change, resolution change; R-003). Celeb-DF++ adds H.264 c35 and c45 as extra compression levels (C3-04). The FF++ paper words c23 as a constant rate quantization parameter and Celeb-DF++ does not define c35 and c45, so mapping them onto the CRF scale of the lab studies is an assumption. If they are CRF-like: c23 sits where lab studies already show clear SNR loss, and c35, c40 and c45 are beyond the CRF 30 level where pulse-rate error was roughly 7 bpm or worse in lab video (C3-10). Whether the deepfake corpora keep a usable pulse is not answered by these papers; R-008 measured it directly on FF++ and found the FF++ signal quality very low. A direct, cheap check (ffprobe bit rates and resolution of the actual files, and POS SNR on real videos only) is the missing evidence and needs no GPU.

### B3. rPPG on current (2024 to 2026) generators

- Cross-generator, talking-face, identity-disjoint (R-009 and C3-15): an rPPG-derived 1D ResNet scores AUC about 0.69 to 0.99 depending on generator, and the leave-one-generator-out mean is 0.752 (per held-out generator 0.554 to 0.865). So the signal differs strongly by generator and is not reliable on at least one (IP-LAP, near chance when held out).
- The authors themselves caution that the signal is "rPPG-derived" with no proof of cardiac origin, and list confounds: real 30 fps versus fake 24 to 25 fps, real videos windowed along their length versus one window per fake, and resolution or encoding differences (C3-15). These confounds are specific, checkable, and bear directly on O-010 (d).
- Constructed fakes with ground truth (C3-16): face swap keeps most of the target's pulse (heart-rate MAE 3.13 to 5.70 bpm), motion transfer destroys it (12.51 bpm), and simple thresholds on waveform discrepancy separate real from motion-transfer fakes (AUC 0.934) far better than real from face swap (AUC 0.655), within-dataset. Detection of constructed fakes: rPPG-only AUC 89.27 (face swap) and 95.29 (motion transfer), subject-disjoint 5-fold, in-dataset. Consistent with R-008 (modern face swaps still carry a plausible pulse that follows the driving video).
- Cross-dataset (C3-17, R-007 context): a map-based rPPG classifier trained on FF++ plus spoofing data stays under 30 percent TPR at 10 percent FPR on DFDC and Celeb-DF v2 (C3-17 joint-training protocol); DeepRhythm trained on FF++ and tested on DFDC-preview gets accuracy 0.641 (C3-05); DeepFakesON-Phys falls from in-dataset 0.999 to 0.622 on talking-face fakes (R-009 v1 numbers).
- Net: evidence is consistent with D-006 as written (candidate, not adopted). Nothing found contradicts the plan to test rather than assume. A single 2026 single-author preprint claims an rPPG gain inside a larger detector (C3-18); it is not usable as evidence.

### B4. Where the "pulse" signal may not be a pulse

- DeepRhythm's plain spatial-temporal map is at chance (about 0.5 accuracy) on every FF++ test subset, and the gain comes from motion magnification plus attention (C3-05).
- C3-15 states explicitly that its discriminative content may not be cardiac; R-009 reports a per-generator spread; the frame-rate gap between real and fake videos is a mechanical difference that can produce separation with no physiology involved.
- Mirsky and Lee argue a generator could be trained against a biological-signal discriminator (C3-01), and R-010 shows pulse editing (EUSIPCO 2023); a detector using rPPG may be evaded.
- Implication for O-010 (d): the negative-control list can be built from these sources: time-shuffled and cross-video-shuffled windows, real videos re-encoded to the fake pipeline's frame rate, resolution and codec, one fixed number of windows per video on both sides, and a classical-method (POS) check next to the learned extractor.

### B5. Skin tone and lighting

- Cross-dataset, heart-rate MAE by Fitzpatrick type (C3-14, VitalVideo VV100): roughly 0 to 0.7 bpm for type 1 versus about 11 to 17 bpm for type 6 across nine model and training-set combinations; training skin-tone composition matters.
- POS paper (C3-08) built a 15-subject skin-tone set (types III to V) for benchmarking, so its design assumed skin tone is a real factor; R-009 and C3-15 do not stratify by skin tone.
- Celeb-DF real videos: 88.1 percent Caucasian, 5.1 percent Asian, 6.8 percent African American as printed for the original set (C3-04); a skin-tone stratified analysis on that set will have very few cells.

### B6. Validation datasets for the extractor (O-010 (a)) and access facts

- UBFC-rPPG (dataset web page, not the dataset paper): Logitech C920 webcam, 30 fps, 640x480, uncompressed 8-bit RGB, CMS50E oximeter ground truth; dataset 1 has 8 videos (about 16,500 frames) and dataset 2 has 42 shared videos (of 46) with a time-sensitive maths game; the page asks users to cite the associated paper and gives a download link; I found no licence text in the page extract. C3-16 states that it obtained authorisation for UBFC-rPPG.
- PURE (dataset web page of TU Ilmenau, not the RO-MAN 2014 paper): 10 people (8 male, 2 female), 6 setups, 60 one-minute sequences, 30 Hz, 640x480, finger-clip oximeter at 60 Hz, all at rest; datasets are distributed on request by e-mail to the dataset address; the page says it only accepts requests from academic institutions (not from free-mail addresses) and does not accept company requests because of licensing restrictions. If the project has only free-mail addresses, access may need a supervisor or institutional address: flag for the user.
- COHFACE: 160 one-minute videos, 20 fps, 640x480, MPEG-4 at about 0.25 Mb/s (C3-07, C3-12); C3-16 says it was used under its research-access conditions.
- UCLA-rPPG: 98 subjects, 489 videos, uncompressed, diverse skin tones (C3-12); access terms not seen.
- No dataset paper was opened (UBFC and PURE paper NR), so no access term is attributed to a paper; the facts above come from the dataset pages and the benchmark papers.

### B7. Discrepancies found while reading (not resolved)

1. R-009's source (arXiv 2607.21776) lists only version v1 on arXiv (checked 2026-10-24). The later paper C3-15 cites it under the title "PulseGuard: rPPG-derived temporal signals as a forensic modality for talking-face deepfake detection" and quotes AUC 0.826, DeepFakesON-Phys 0.576 and 20,279 fake videos, whereas the v1 text in the project's scratch folder has AUC 0.806, DeepFakesON-Phys 0.622 and 17,500 fakes. Until one version is shown to be the source of each number, quote neither beyond "as printed in the v1 file".
2. Tolosana et al. (C3-02) give 890 real videos for Celeb-DF; the dataset papers give 590.
3. DeepRhythm's file contains "Anon." running headers next to the ACM Multimedia '20 header; the venue is probably right but the file may be a pre-camera-ready layout.

### B8. Implications for the open design choices (no choice is made here)

- O-010 (a): the evidence offers three ways to get pulse ground truth: construct fakes on rPPG datasets that already have it (C3-16 style), use own studio data with ECG or PPG (R-008), or check extractor behaviour on real videos only by SNR with no ground truth. Window and frame-rate needs in B1 are small enough that POS on CPU and a 160-frame network window both fit inside 10 s clips.
- O-010 (b): identity-disjoint split, several generator families, leave-one-generator-out reporting, 5 seeds are all done in C3-15; matching its design makes the project result comparable with that line.
- O-010 (c): C3-16 reports an AUC gain when fusing rPPG tokens with behaviour tokens (92.80 versus 89.27 rPPG alone), but the interval is wide (+- 5.65 and +- 4.34 over folds); this shows the size of effect the paired-difference criterion in O-010 will have to resolve.
- O-010 (d): see B4.
- Compression handling: matching or measuring compression between real and fake videos and between train and test is needed; C3-12 shows mismatch hurts.
